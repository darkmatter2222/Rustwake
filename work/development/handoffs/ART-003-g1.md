## 3. Plate floor / dead-black band — the binding constraint

The phone fit is `min(4, 390 / (WORLD_W + 8))`, so `scale = 1.95` and the world paints 436.8 CSS px of the 844 px viewport (**51.8 %**), centred in the chrome band (148.6 px of clear band above and below, exactly equal). The arithmetic ceiling of that term is the zero-gutter case `224 / 192 × 390 = 455 px = 53.9 %`, so **≥ 60 % of the viewport is not obtainable** while the whole 224-unit-tall world must stay on screen — reaching it would need the dock or the wreck to leave the visible band, which L-1 forbids. Within that ceiling the band is now both larger and centred; the remaining dead-black share is the night backdrop itself (`#0b1220` + floor `#22384a` both land in bucket 0), which is why bucket 0 moves only as fast as the plate grows: 96.5 → 94.8 %.

## 7. Limitations, honestly stated

1. **A3-2 raters**: both passes were the same single model reading the crops in two different orders (file order, then reversed), not two independent human raters. The `method`/`limitation` fields in `…-naming.json` say so.
2. **Plate share** tops out at 51.8 % (§3); if the product owner wants a larger plate, the world height or the chrome insets have to change, which is a different ticket.
3. **Per-cell ink density** is roughly flat (±2 pp) while the absolute ink rises, because the same glyphs are painted into a 16.6 % larger cell area; the graded totals are the px and unit² rows in A3-4.
4. The sheet's first band keeps the literal 1.8056 by design (row comparability), so the sheet census is not a second measurement of the phone fit.
5. The clip instrument changed shape-matching from literals to `fitCamera`-derived sizes; its base run (26/26, mean step 76.38) is reproduced with the same instrument on both trees, so the two are directly comparable.
